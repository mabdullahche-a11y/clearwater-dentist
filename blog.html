How to clean between your teeth

A toothbrush cleans the surfaces it can reach. Floss or an interdental brush helps reach the areas where teeth meet. Try cleaning between your teeth once a day, at a time you can stick with.

Guide the floss gently between two teeth. Curve it around the side of one tooth and slide it up and down, then repeat against its neighbour. Avoid snapping the floss into your gums.

If you have a bridge, braces or larger gaps, ask your dentist to show you a tool that fits. Mention bleeding that keeps coming back.

Caring for your child’s first teeth

Start brushing when the first tooth appears. Use a soft child-sized brush and a tiny smear of fluoride toothpaste for children under three. For ages three to six, use a pea-sized amount and supervise brushing.

Book a first dental visit when the first tooth comes through or by the first birthday. Early visits help you learn how to care for new teeth and let your child get familiar with the dental setting.

Help with brushing until your child can reach every surface reliably. If brushing feels like a daily struggle, tell your dentist what’s hard and ask for a demonstration.

What to expect at a first visit

The visit usually begins with a conversation about your health, previous dental treatment and what you want help with. Bring a list of medications and allergies, and any recent dental X-rays you already have.

Your dentist checks your teeth, gums and mouth. They may suggest X-rays if those would help assess a concern. Ask them to explain what they see and show you where the problem is, if possible.

Before you leave, ask which next steps are time-sensitive and what costs to expect. If you’re nervous, say so at the start and agree on how to pause during the exam.

Three common root canal myths

“A root canal always hurts.” Local anaesthetic is used during treatment. Your experience can vary, so tell the dentist if you feel discomfort and ask what to expect afterwards.

“Removing the tooth is always simpler.” Extraction and root canal treatment solve different problems. Ask whether the tooth can be restored and what keeping or replacing it would involve.

“Treatment ends when the canals are sealed.” The tooth still needs an appropriate final restoration. Ask what type is recommended and when it should be fitted.
